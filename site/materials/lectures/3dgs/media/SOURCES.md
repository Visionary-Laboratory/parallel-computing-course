# Visual input/output media

These assets support the local 3DGS lecture. They are not original course photography or new training results.

## Garden photos and COLMAP reconstruction

- Original scene: Mip-NeRF 360, Barron et al., CVPR 2022: https://jonbarron.info/mipnerf360/
- Distribution used: official Viser COLMAP example: https://viser.studio/main/examples/demos/colmap_visualizer/
- Archive linked by Viser's download script: https://brentyi.github.io/viser-example-assets/colmap_garden.zip
- Download script: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/viser-project/viser/main/examples/assets/download_assets.sh
- Retrieved 2026-11-08. Input archive is not redistributed in this repository.
- `garden-0.jpg`, `garden-1.jpg`, `garden-2.jpg`: unmodified `images_8` files DSC07966.JPG, DSC08135.JPG, DSC07975.JPG respectively.
- `garden-colmap.json`: derived teaching subset, 16,000 sampled points out of 138,766, three selected photograph poses plus a subset of overview cameras. Far points cropped; all positions and orientations receive one common rigid coordinate transformation. Camera intrinsics remain in original-resolution pixel units.
- `garden-colmap.svg` in the generated site is a static drawing of those real reconstruction points and cameras, not a screenshot of the COLMAP GUI.
- Format interpretation: https://colmap.github.io/format.html
- Preserve original dataset attribution; no new license is asserted for the third-party photos/data.

## Original 3DGS paper figures

Bernhard Kerbl, Georgios Kopanas, Thomas Leimkühler, George Drettakis, *3D Gaussian Splatting for Real-Time Radiance Field Rendering*, SIGGRAPH 2023. Paper version: https://arxiv.org/html/2308.04079v1 (CC BY 4.0, as stated by arXiv).

- `gaussians-paper-fig3.png`: original figure 3, unchanged. Train scene, showing rendering and Gaussian visualization with reduced scale. https://arxiv.org/html/2308.04079v1/figures/anisotropic/real2.png
- `garden-render.png`: original Garden rendering, unchanged, shown in the paper's results comparison. https://arxiv.org/html/2308.04079v1/results2/garden/ours/DSC07964.png
- These are different scenes/views as labeled in the lesson. No inference that the illustrative Gaussian anatomy corresponds to these exact trained parameters is intended.

The single-Gaussian anatomy SVG is original course artwork generated by `figures.py`.

## Garden scene as ellipsoids and splats (current introduction)

- Source: original GraphDeco / Kerbl et al. SIBR real-time viewer demonstration, linked from https://github.com/graphdeco-inria/gaussian-splatting#running-the-real-time-viewer
- Original video: https://github.com/graphdeco-inria/gaussian-splatting/assets/40643808/0940547f-1d82-4c2f-a616-44eabbf0f816
- `garden-ellipsoids.png`: full, unchanged video frame extracted at 11 seconds (Ellipsoids mode).
- `garden-splats.png`: full, unchanged video frame extracted at 8 seconds (Splats mode).
- Both frames show the stationary Garden camera labelled 107 in the viewer. The course uses SVG viewports to display the table/vase region (x=260, y=335, w=680, h=375) and a table detail (x=390, y=475, w=265, h=190), keeping application panels outside the displayed region. It does not invent or reconstruct image content.
- The spatial diagram is original course artwork. The camera/frustum arrangement is schematic, not a recovered pose. The virtual image plane is in front of the camera. The exact same source view is used for the depicted ellipsoid scene and composited image.
- Related primary teaching reference: Bernhard Kerbl, *3DGS in Practice*, 3DV 2024 tutorial, slide 30, uses a Garden Splats/Ellipsoids comparison: https://3dgstutorial.github.io/3dv_part2.pdf . No tutorial slide is redistributed here.
- Keep original author attribution; no additional license is asserted for the demonstration frames. Used in the local, unpublished teaching draft.

## 2D Gaussian construction film

- `gaussian-build.mp4` and its poster/final/key-frame JPEGs are generated by `../make_gaussian_film.py` for this course. Target image: `garden-0.jpg`, attributed to Mip-NeRF 360 above.
- The left image is numerically composited from explicitly computed 2D Gaussian footprints on a white background. The separate target thumbnail is labelled. The final reconstruction never blends in or switches to the target photograph.
- The film illustrates four 2D Gaussian parameters and accumulation into a recognizable image. It is a greedy 2D image approximation, not recovered 3D Gaussian scene parameters or a recording of 3DGS training.
- `gaussian-build.json` records the source hash, fitting resolution, number of Gaussians and measured reconstruction error. Rebuilding the site copies the already encoded video; it does not rerun fitting or encoding.
